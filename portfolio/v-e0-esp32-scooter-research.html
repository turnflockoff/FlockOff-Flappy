V-E0 Shared-Ride Scooter Electronics

ESP32 dashboard research · TurnFlockOff · Project date:

Project status: Research and conceptual design only. “V-E0” is a neutral project codename. No affiliation, sponsorship, endorsement, or authorization by any manufacturer or operator is claimed. A codename does not itself eliminate trademark or other legal risk.

This project documents a visual investigation of a shared-ride scooter/e-bike display and control electronics assembly, then explores whether selected dashboard and communications functions could eventually be handled by an independent ESP32-based board.

Hypothesis / reasoning

The original display/control assembly may combine user display, low-voltage power regulation, external I/O, serial communications, and cellular/GPS-related hardware. If connector pin functions, electrical levels, protocols, battery limits, and mechanical dimensions can be measured, an independent ESP32-S3 dashboard/interface may be feasible. This remains a hypothesis.

What I did

  • Organized 43 original hardware/document photographs and 9 engineering-artwork images.
  • Reviewed the enclosure, multi-board electronics assembly, connector panel, PCB surfaces, and battery label.
  • Recorded nine visible connector labels: USB DEBUG, BRAKE, THROTTLE, SPEAKER, LED, DATA, 5V OUTPUT, 12V OUTPUT, and FRONT LIGHT.
  • Transcribed visible PCB markings where legible and documented uncertainties.
  • Outlined an ESP32-S3 dashboard/interface architecture and created conceptual PCB/enclosure illustrations.

How I did it

Visual inspection of supplied photographs, transcription of visible labels, evidence organization, subsystem mapping, and conceptual engineering illustration. Candidate functions were grouped into power, display, communications, sensor/input, and lighting/audio interfaces.

What happened / observed result

The evidence supports a preliminary research direction: a multi-board display/control assembly is visible, the connector panel carries nine functional labels, and PCB silkscreen suggests multiple power and signal domains. A battery label photograph visibly reads 46.8 V, 15 Ah, and 702 Wh, while smaller configuration text remains unclear.

Not demonstrated: verified connector pinout, measured voltage table, decoded protocol, completed KiCad schematic, manufacturing-ready PCB, firmware integration, fabricated replacement board, successful bench test, or powered vehicle test.

Concept-art and CAD disclaimer

All generated wiring diagrams, exploded views, 3D renders, PCB illustrations, component placements, connector geometries, and depicted dimensions are conceptual illustrations only. They are not measured CAD models, verified mechanical assemblies, validated electrical schematics, manufacturing-ready PCB files, Gerbers, or fabrication instructions. Rendered dimensions are placeholders unless independently measured.

What I would change next

  1. Capture square-on board photos with a scale.
  2. Confirm battery chemistry, series count, BMS limits, and maximum charge voltage.
  3. Identify the exact motor controller and permitted voltage range.
  4. Measure board, enclosure, mounting, and connector geometry.
  5. Characterize connector pins with safe, isolated, current-limited test equipment.
  6. Capture communications passively before attempting an interface.
  7. Create a measured KiCad schematic and PCB layout.
  8. Build a low-voltage bench prototype without throttle, brake, or traction control.
